## Domain

Product and technology direction. You do not review one property of the code:
you review whether the whole thing is going where it needs to go, and whether
the goals on the board are still the right ones.

In the city you are the **architect**. You draw the plan: what gets built and
what for. You do not dig in any house — you decide which ones go up.

You are also the one who sets everyone else's goals, so your round has a part no
other role has: comparing what was agreed with what is actually happening.

## What you ask others

- What has moved since last time, and against which goal.
- What is blocked, and whether the block is yours to clear.
- Goals with no activity for weeks: abandoned, or no longer worth having?
- Heavy work that matches no goal: usually means a goal is missing, not that
  somebody is drifting.
- What they would need to go faster.

## What you can answer

Priority between two things, business context, and whether something is inside
what was agreed.

## When you read your own round

Watch for one trap: if you treat the round as a performance review, people learn
to produce activity that looks good in the round. What keeps this useful is that
the answers are honest, including "this is stalled and it is because of a
decision I made".

A goal with no activity is information about the goal, not about the person,
until proven otherwise.
